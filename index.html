Kawman 1 — Coastal

Logistics, Aquaculture, Sama Dilaut Community, Port of Isabela

Key Validation Question Does the community experience the utang (advance financing) trap as a structural constraint, or as individual financial mismanagement? The answer reveals whether conscientisation has occurred.

K1.1 — Photovoice Prosperity Mapping

Prosperity domain lens: Foundations of Prosperity (material security) + Belonging, Identities & Culture (Sama Dilaut sea-based identity).

SHOWed — Sample Caption: "Sama stilt houses at dawn"

SHOWedSample CaptionConv./Div.?
S — SeeWooden stilt houses over shallow water. Kumpit fishing boats tied alongside. Children on the walkway. Mangroves visible in the background. Solar lamp on one post — the only light source.
H — HappeningA Sama Dilaut family begins the fishing day. The houses are built over water because the community holds no land title — the sea is literally their home, not a metaphor.
O — Our livesProsperity here means: access to the sea, proximity to the mangrove nursery, freedom to fish. But it is fragile — no electricity, no potable water, no recognised tenure, no safety net if the fish run out.
W — WhySama Dilaut are historically marginalised even within Basilan's IP communities. They hold no CADT. Port modernisation and reclamation projects threaten their grounds. The LGU's CLUP does not recognise their stilt settlements as permanent.
ed — DoAdvocate inclusion in the CADT process or an alternative tenure instrument. Map fishing grounds and mangrove stewardship zones into CEESAM. Propose a co-managed MPA recognising traditional use rights. Engage MMWA (Marang-Marang Women's Assoc.) as model.

K1.2 — Root Cause Tree

LayerPre-filled Answer (K1)
TRUNKDespite Fisheries LQ of 3.84 (highly specialised), most catch value leaves Isabela within hours of landing. Fishers remain poor while the resource is abundant.
Branch 1 (Effect)Fishers earn ₱150–300/day despite catching fish worth ₱2,000+ at Zamboanga wholesale — a 5–10× value capture gap.
Branch 2 (Effect)Youth out-migration — young Sama and Tausug leave because 'fishing doesn't pay'. Marine knowledge is being lost with each generation.
Branch 3 (Effect)Ecological pressure — fishers increase volume to compensate for low price, depleting nearshore stocks. No MPA or fishing calendar exists.
Root 1 (Cause)No cold chain — without ice or cold storage, fishers must sell within hours to whoever is at the port. This creates a monopsony market with only 3–4 buyers.
Root 2 (Cause)Trader advance financing (utang system) — fishers receive fuel and gear money in advance from a specific buyer, then are pre-committed to sell exclusively to that buyer regardless of price.
Root 3 (Cause)No Halal-certified processing facility — despite Isabela being a majority-Muslim city, it cannot access the Sabah and BIMP-EAGA Halal export premium markets.
Root 4 (Cause)No community-based fisheries management plan or formalised MPA. No formal marine stewardship structure for Kawman 1 coastal waters.
Root 5 (Cause)Sama Dilaut have no CADT and no formal land/sea tenure. Port reclamation projects proceed without IP FPIC consultation.

K1.3 — Iceberg Model

LevelPre-filled Answer (K1 — fisheries value leakage)
EVENTIn January 2026, sardine farmgate price dropped 40% overnight when two Zamboanga-based buyers temporarily withdrew from the Port of Isabela landing site.
PATTERNThis is the third such price collapse in five years. Each follows the same sequence: buyer withdrawal → panic selling → price floor disappears → recovery takes 2–3 months. Each collapse is more severe than the last.
STRUCTUREMonopsony market structure — 3–4 major buyers control the port landing site. Trader advance financing (utang) locks fishers into exclusive selling relationships. No cold chain means fishers cannot hold stock to wait for better prices. No cooperative aggregation or auction system. No formal MPA so no fishing calendar to manage seasonal supply.
MENTAL MODEL'Kailangan dumaan sa Zamboanga — walang ibang paraan.' The belief that Isabela can only be a transit point, not a processing hub, is held by fishers AND by LGU planners. This shapes every investment decision.
LEVERAGEThe mental model is the highest leverage point. Once fishers and LGU planners shift from 'transit point' to 'processing hub' framing, investments in cold chain and cooperative buying make sense.

K1.4 — Causal Loop Diagram

LoopDescription (K1)
R− The Utang TrapLow catch price → fisher borrows from trader for fuel/gear → fisher must sell exclusively to trader → trader sets price → low catch price. This is a vicious reinforcing loop. Each cycle deepens debt and dependency.
R− Ecological DepletionLow income → fish more to compensate → nearshore stock depletes → fish harder to catch → lower income. Compounds with the utang trap.
B ConstraintBFAR vessel monitoring + PCG presence creates some limit on overfishing, but enforcement is inconsistent and underfunded.
R+ Virtuous Cycle PotentialCooperative fuel fund → fishers not obligated to single buyer → price competition between buyers → higher farmgate price → fishers invest in better gear → higher quality catch → higher price. Cold chain enables fishers to hold stock for 24 hours, breaking the 'must sell now' constraint.
Data LinkMarine output gap: 3,200 MT inflow / 2,100 MT outflow. The 1,100 MT difference is value extracted by the trader network before it leaves Isabela. If 50% of that could be locally processed, it represents a significant GVA gain.

K1.5 — IAD Framework

IAD ElementPre-filled Answer (K1 — fisheries commons)
Biophysical ConditionsNearshore marine waters of Isabela Bay and Basilan Strait. Mangrove forest (19 species including endangered/near-threatened). Coral reef (degraded). Seagrass beds. Fisheries LQ 3.84. ~3,200 MT marine output. Malamawi Island mariculture potential. Climate risk: Kawman 1 at ~4.6m elevation — high sea-level rise vulnerability.
Attributes of CommunitySama Dilaut (Badjao), Tausug, Bajau: multi-ethnic coastal community. Strong kinship networks. High mutual dependence on marine resources. Low formal education. Trust in traditional leaders but distrust of external regulators. High inequality between fishers and traders. MMWA a functioning cooperative with ecotourism capability.
Rules-in-useFORMAL: RA 8550 (Fisheries Code); BFAR regulations; PPA port rules; RA 8371 (IPRA); LGU fisheries ordinance. CUSTOMARY: Sama sea-territory norms; utang system as de facto access rule; Tausug elder arbitration. GAP: no community-based MPA; no FPIC protocol for port expansion; no Sama Dilaut tenure instrument.
Action SituationsDaily: where to fish, to whom to sell, whether to invest in gear. Seasonal: whether to shift to seaweed cultivation during monsoon. Strategic: whether to join cooperative or remain in utang system. Crisis: when buyer withdraws, who do you call? Currently: no collective response mechanism.
Outcomes (Current)High extraction, low value capture. Ecological degradation. Youth out-migration. Sama Dilaut tenure insecurity. No income diversification.
Outcomes (Desired)Managed fishery with MPA. Cold chain and Halal processing cooperative. Ecotourism diversification (MMWA model). Sama Dilaut sea tenure recognised. Youth returning with marine stewardship skills.
Evaluative CriteriaEquity (who benefits?); Sustainability (is the fishery regenerating?); Efficiency (is value captured locally?); Legitimacy (do fishers trust the governance?); Cultural fit (does it respect Sama/Tausug sea-territory norms and adat?); Accountability.

Kawman 2 — CBD

Finance, Administration, Trade & Services Hub

Key Validation Question Who benefits from the 1.47 services multiplier — formal sector only, or also informal vendors and cooperatives? The answer reveals how inclusive (or exclusive) the city's economic growth actually is.

K2.1 — Photovoice Prosperity Mapping

SHOWed — Sample Caption: "Cooperative building with queue of members"

SHOWedSample Caption✓/✗
S — SeeTwo-storey cooperative building in Isabela Proper. A queue of people — mostly women — waiting to transact. Signage in Chavacano, Tagalog, and English. A security guard at the door.
H — HappeningMembers accessing micro-loans and savings services. The cooperative is one of few financial institutions serving the informal sector without requiring formal employment documentation.
O — Our livesThe Trade & Services multiplier of 1.47 runs through institutions like this. When the cooperative lends ₱100, it generates ₱147 in total economic activity. But the queue shows demand vastly exceeds what the cooperative can serve.
W — WhyCommercial banks require collateral most residents cannot provide. The cooperative fills the gap — but is undercapitalised. Islamic finance options are growing but still limited and not well-publicised.
ed — DoSupport cooperative capitalisation through BARMM's Special Development Fund. Develop an Islamic finance product (via MLAP or OFI) specifically for micro-enterprises. Include cooperative members in Pentahelix Action Workshop (07B).

K2.2 — Root Cause Tree / Iceberg

LayerPre-filled Answer (K2 — financial exclusion)
TRUNKDespite the services sector dominating the city's GDP at 44.7% and a Trade & Services multiplier of 1.47, financial services do not reach the informal sector — which is most of the workforce.
Branches (Effects)Informal sector excluded from credit and insurance. Women's enterprises disproportionately excluded. Multiplier benefits formal businesses but not poorest households. Cooperative unable to meet demand.
Roots (Causes)Commercial banks require collateral unavailable to informal workers. Cooperative undercapitalised (demand > capital). Islamic finance products (MLAP) exist but are not widely accessible or promoted. LGU data systems cannot measure or track the informal sector.
MENTAL MODEL'The informal sector is not our mandate.' The LGU sees cooperatives as private-sector matters, not public infrastructure. Until cooperatives are treated as public financial infrastructure (like roads or water), they will remain underfunded and underpowered.
STRUCTURENo dedicated BARMM fund for cooperative capitalisation targeted at Isabela. No LGU cooperative development officer. No Islamic microfinance bureau in the city. Banks' community reinvestment obligations not enforced locally.

Respondent / CohortExpected Direction
KII: Mayor's Office / CPDOProud of services sector growth and cooperative network. May not be aware of the capital gap depth. Open to BARMM SDF proposal if framed as economic efficiency (multiplier return on investment).
KII: Basilan Chamber of CommerceAware of Islamic finance gap. See BIMP-EAGA trade corridor as opportunity. May resist cooperative scale-up if they perceive it as competition.

FGD C4 Adult Women (CBD)Livelihood via sari-sari store or home-based food processing. Credit access is essential but often insufficient. Women aware of cooperative queue problem. Strong aspiration for children's education at BaSC.

IAD ElementK2 Pre-fill
BiophysicalDense urban built environment. Port interface at its western edge. River system (flood risk). Limited open space. High commercial footprint. 100% diesel electricity grid — every power outage hits CBD commerce immediately.
Community AttributesMulti-ethnic (Tausug, Yakan, Chavacano, Chinese-Filipino, Sama). Formal and informal employment. High transient population (port workers, traders). Strong mosque network. Cooperative membership ~15,000 (estimated). Active Basilan Chamber.

Kawman 3 — East

Urban Expansion, Construction Demand, Residential Workforce Zone

Key Validation Question When the community talks about 'development', do they mean infrastructure serving them, or infrastructure changing who can afford to live here? The tension between 'growth' and 'displacement' is the K3 mental model to surface.

K3.1 — Photovoice & Root Cause

SHOWed — Sample Caption: "Water truck parked outside a new housing block"

SHOWedSample Caption✓/✗
S — SeeA water delivery truck beside a newly built residential subdivision. Residents queue with containers. Children helping carry water inside. No piped water connection to the building visible.
H — HappeningDespite this being a formally approved subdivision, there is no piped water supply. Residents pay ₱50–80 per 20-litre container from a water truck. A family of 5 may spend ₱300–400/week on water alone.
O — Our livesThis is what 'development' looks like for families who moved to Kawman 3 for affordable housing. The house is there. The water is not. Power outages 4–6 hours daily. This is not prosperity — it is a different kind of poverty.
W — WhyThe CPDO approves new subdivisions without mandatory utility capacity certification. BASELCO's grid is 100% diesel with no expansion budget. The water system was designed for 2010 population levels. There is no solar RE facility yet operational.
ed — DoRequire utility capacity certification as a precondition for subdivision approval (CPDO ordinance). Fast-track the solar RE facility for Kawman 3/4. Advocate for a water system expansion budget in the next LGU investment programme (BDIP).

Iceberg Analysis (K3)

LayerPre-filled Answer (K3)
TRUNKUrbanisation in Kawman 3 is outpacing utility and service infrastructure — creating a new form of poverty in formally 'developed' areas.
EventsSunrise brgy: 12-hr water outages during March 2026 peak demand. Brownouts 4–6 hrs/day. New SMEs shutting down or moving.
PatternInfrastructure complaints increase each dry season. New subdivisions approved annually but no new utility capacity. CPDO approvals are not cross-checked with BASELCO or water district data.
StructureBASELCO 100% diesel — no baseload expansion. Water system: 2010 design capacity. CPDO: no mandatory utility certification. CLUP aspirational but no utility backbone budget.
Mental Model'Ang development ay pagpapatayo ng bahay.' (Development means building houses.) The CPDO's performance is measured in approved permits — not in utility coverage. Construction IS the goal, not the means.

K3.2 — KII & FGD Key Themes

Respondent / CohortExpected Direction
KII: CPDOMay frame utility gaps as 'BASELCO's problem, not CPDO's'. Useful to probe: 'What happens to a subdivision approval if BASELCO says the grid can't support it?' If the answer is 'we approve anyway', that is the structural gap.
KII: BASELCO / Water DistrictCan confirm grid capacity data. Likely to express frustration at being blamed for an underfunded situation. They are a symptom, not the cause. Key ally for the solar RE proposal.
FGD C4 Adult WomenMost likely to speak about daily utility management — fetching water, managing brownouts, adjusting cooking schedules. Their coping strategies are invisible in CPDO data but visible in Photovoice.
FGD C3 Young Women 15–24Balancing cost of living in Kawman 3 vs. economic opportunity in Kawman 2. Job access from residential zone. Transportation cost. Safety on commute to CBD.
FGD C5 Adult MenSME operators: brownout cost (lost refrigeration, halted production, generator fuel). Awareness of solar alternatives but no access. BRICKS programme awareness?

K3.3 — Governance & IAD

IAD ElementK3 Pre-fill
BiophysicalFlat coastal fringes transitioning to rolling terrain. River corridors (flood risk). Urbanising former agricultural land. No designated green space in new subdivisions. High construction materials demand — ~7,000 MT pure inflow.
CommunityDiverse — mix of long-term rural families and new urban migrants from within Basilan. Rapid population growth. Multiple ethnolinguistic groups. High renters vs. owners ratio in new subdivisions.
Rules-in-useFORMAL: LGU subdivision approval; HLURB/DHSUD standards (formally required but locally unenforced); CPDO zoning. CUSTOMARY: informal land subdivisions pre-dating formal system; barangay-level informal rental agreements. GAP: no utility capacity ordinance; no green space percentage requirement.
Key Governance GapNo inter-agency coordination mechanism between CPDO, BASELCO, Water District, and BDIP investment planning. Each agency plans in silos. The solar RE investment must be cross-departmental.

Kawman 4 — West

Industrial Core — Rubber & Coconut Manufacturing, BRICKS Circular Economy Zone

Key Validation Question Does the community in Kawman 4 (workers, enterprise operators) know what the rubber multiplier of 1.76 means in practice — that each ₱1M processed locally generates ₱1.76M in economic activity? If not, the multiplier narrative has not reached ground level. Test this.

K4.1 — Photovoice & Root Cause

SHOWed — Sample Caption: "Rubber collection point. Bags loaded onto truck, heading toward port."

SHOWedSample Caption✓/✗
S — SeeLarge white bags of cuplump rubber stacked at a roadside collection point in Menzi. A Zamboanga-registered truck being loaded. Farmers standing to the side, watching.
H — HappeningRaw rubber leaving Isabela as a commodity. The value-added step — converting cuplump to crumb rubber or concentrated latex — will happen in Zamboanga or Davao. The jobs, the income, the multiplier: all leave on this truck.
O — Our livesThe multiplier of 1.76 means that ₱1M in processed rubber generates ₱1.76M in local economic activity — jobs, services, food purchases, school fees. That multiplier is currently happening in Zamboanga, not here.
W — WhyProcessing requires reliable electricity. BASELCO's diesel grid makes processing costs unviable. There is no crumb rubber plant. The Kapayawan-to-port corridor adds 11–15% transport cost. Dual regulatory friction (Region IX + BARMM) discourages investors.
ed — DoSolar RE facility in Maligue/Cabunbata area makes processing viable. BARMM-MAFAR and DTI joint incentive package for a crumb rubber anchor tenant. BRICKS eco-hub converts processing waste to construction materials. Farm-to-market road upgrade along Kapayawan corridor.

Causal Loop Diagram (K4)

CLD LoopK4 Description
R+ Virtuous Cycle (if solar RE unlocked)Solar RE investment → lower energy cost → rubber/coconut processing viable → crumb rubber plant opens → jobs in Kawman 4 → wages spent in Kawman 2/3 (multiplier activates) → tax revenue → further infrastructure investment. Each ₱1M processed = ₱1.76M output, ₱760K additional economic activity.
R− Current Vicious CycleHigh diesel cost → processing unviable → raw export continues → industrial zone underemployed → investment doesn't come → BASELCO has no revenue to modernise grid → high diesel cost.
B ConstraintBASELCO franchise area; dual jurisdiction (Region IX/BARMM); land assembly for Maligue solar farm requires IP and LGU consultation; global rubber price volatility.
Intervention PrioritySolar RE breaks both loops simultaneously — it makes processing viable AND gives BASELCO revenue to expand capacity. This is the single highest-impact infrastructure intervention in the city.

K4.2 — IAD Framework

IAD ElementK4 Pre-fill
BiophysicalFlat coastal plains and western fringes suitable for warehouses and processing plants. BRICKS programme testing eco-hubs (rubber waste → construction materials). Maligue designated for solar-powered processing. Kabunbata logistics node connecting upland to port. 100% diesel grid is the binding biophysical constraint.
CommunityMix of agricultural smallholders (rubber/coconut farmers), industrial workers, urban-rural fringe residents. Barangay Menzi has historical rubber processing heritage. San Rafael growing commercial-residential. High awareness of Zamboanga as alternative livelihood destination.
Rules-in-useFORMAL: LGU industrial zone classification; DTI investment registration; BASELCO franchise; Region IX-BARMM boundary agreement. CUSTOMARY: Farm gate price negotiations via trader network; informal weighing systems; credit-in-kind from input traders. GAP: no environmental compliance certification requirement for small processors; no single-stop-shop permit for industrial zone.
Action SituationsDaily: farmers decide whether to sell fresh cuplump or hold. Weekly: price negotiation with trader. Seasonal: harvest cycle; typhoon-proofing stored rubber. Strategic: whether to invest in small processing equipment vs. remain a raw supplier. Investment decision: solar vs. diesel for operations.
Outcomes (Desired)Crumb rubber plant operational in Kawman 4. Solar RE reducing processing costs. BRICKS eco-hub converting waste to construction materials. Farm-to-market road upgraded. Local employment rising. Multiplier 1.76 activating within Isabela, not Zamboanga.

K4.3 — KII & FGD Key Themes

Respondent / CohortExpected Direction
KII: Business (rubber processor / Basilan Chamber)Aware of the processing gap. Will cite energy cost as the blocking factor. Open to solar RE if feasibility study is credible. May know of prior failed processing plant attempts — ask why they failed.
KII: DTI / BARMM-MinDAWill reference BIMP-EAGA Green Industrial Hub concept. May have data on investor inquiries. The dual-jurisdiction question is the key institutional bottleneck — probe this.
KII: CAgO (rubber/coconut extension)Can give production data by barangay. Will know farmer attitudes toward collective processing vs. individual sale. Ask: 'Has anyone tried to start a processing cooperative here before?'
FGD C7 Enterprise OperatorsFrustration with raw export model is likely but may be expressed as resignation: 'That's just how it is.' Push gently toward W (Why does this exist?) — that is where the structural insight lives.

Kawman 5 — Upland

Resource Base — Yakan IP Community, Subsistence Agriculture, 2 Pending CADTs

Key Validation Question Does the Yakan council of elders distinguish between their land (which they manage under adat) and the government's land classification (which may not recognise their rights)? This distinction is the foundation of everything else in Kawman 5.

K5.1 — Photovoice Prosperity Mapping

SHOWed — Sample Caption: "Yakan woman weaving on backstrap loom under coconut tree"

SHOWedSample Caption✓/✗
S — SeeMiddle-aged Yakan woman, sitting on woven mat, working a backstrap loom. Traditional tengkapung (headcloth) motif visible. Behind her: coconut palms, nipa house, a child watching quietly. Morning light.
H — HappeningShe weaves approximately 2 hours per session, twice a week. One piece takes 4 months to complete. This is not spare time — it is deliberate cultural production, alongside farm work, food preparation, and childcare.
O — Our livesYakan weaving is UNESCO-recognised intangible heritage. But the weavers earn less than minimum wage per piece. Abaca fibre must be purchased. Finished textiles are often sold to middlemen in Zamboanga at prices the weaver cannot negotiate.
W — WhyNo cooperative bargaining structure for weavers. The tennun economy is invisible in LGU economic planning — it does not appear in PSA accounts because it is classified as household production, not commercial output. No direct-to-market channel from Isabela.
ed — DoInclude weaving output in CEESAM as a cultural production sector. Propose a Tennun cooperative linked to the Basilan Chamber's Tennun Expo platform. Advocate for Halal-certified cultural enterprise branding. Develop direct-to-market channel bypassing Zamboanga middlemen.

K5.2 — Root Cause Tree & CLD

LayerPre-filled Answer (K5 — vegetable import dependency)
TRUNK95% of vegetables consumed in Isabela are imported from Zamboanga and mainland Mindanao. Local production is negligible despite ~7,000 MT inflow and massive unmet demand.
Branch 1Price spikes during Amihan (NE monsoon) season when supply from Zamboanga drops — poorest households feel it most acutely.
Branch 2Nutrition gap — fresh vegetable access is income-dependent; upland Yakan families eat the least dietary variety.
Branch 3Wealth leaves the community every market day — ₱5,500 MT vegetable inflow vs. <500 MT local outflow represents a substantial daily cash drain from Isabela.
Root 1 (Cause)No irrigation infrastructure — upland farms depend entirely on rain. Kapatagan Grande, the most suitable land for vegetable cultivation, has no functional irrigation system.
Root 2 (Cause)Land tenure insecurity — Yakan farmers without CADT or CLOA titles cannot access formal agricultural credit and avoid investing in permanent crop infrastructure.
Root 3 (Cause)Knowledge gap — lowland vegetable cultivation (tomato, eggplant, pechay, sitaw) is not part of traditional Yakan swidden practice. Training and demo plots are absent.
Root 4 (Cause)No aggregation hub or farm-to-market road at Kapayawan — even when farmers produce surplus, there is no reliable transport system to the CBD market.
CLD R− LoopLow local production → high import dependency → money leaves community → low agricultural investment → low local production. Capturing 20% of the import market = +₱115M/yr local agricultural GVA.
Breaking the LoopSCoPSA (intercropping vegetables under coconut canopy in Kapatagan Grande) + irrigation (BARMM-MAFAR) + aggregation hub at Sumagdang Farm School + CADT fast-track (unlocks credit access and investment willingness).

K5.3 — IAD Framework (Yakan Upland Agricultural Commons)

IAD ElementK5 Pre-fill
BiophysicalMountainous terrain; tropical volcanic soil; high rainfall; coconut-dominant canopy with swidden plots beneath. Watershed areas feeding coastal communities and fisheries. Bamboo and abaca potential. 195,000 gha total city biocapacity. Kapatagan Grande: prime vegetable cultivation potential. Kapayawan: rubber-coconut corridor to port.
CommunityPredominantly Yakan. Small political units led by imam + council of elders. Adat governs land use allocation and rotation. High dependence on subsistence agriculture. 2 CADTs pending for 3 ICCs. Weaving (tennun) as secondary cultural livelihood (2 hrs × 2× week = 4 months per piece). Low cash income but strong social capital.
Rules-in-useFORMAL: IPRA (RA 8371); NCIP ancestral domain process; DENR forest and watershed management; LGU CLUP upland zoning. CUSTOMARY: Yakan adat on land rotation (kaingin calendar); communal forest reserve management; imam authority over boundary disputes. GAP: CADT not yet issued — formal tenure insecurity blocks credit access and investment; LGU CLUP may classify Yakan cultivated land as 'unproductive'.
Action SituationsSeasonal: when to clear, plant, harvest (guided by traditional calendar and Islamic observances — crops planted after Eid al-Fitr). Investment: whether to plant permanent crops (strongly inhibited without CADT). Market: sell copra to local trader or attempt to aggregate through cooperative. Social: whether to allow children to attend lowland school (boarding costs).
Evaluative CriteriaCultural fit (does intervention respect adat and Islamic practice?); equity (does it benefit Yakan families or outside interests?); sustainability (watershed protection); accountability (who decides upland land use?); legitimacy (does the Yakan council endorse it?). Any intervention that bypasses the imam and council of elders will fail regardless of technical merit.

How to Use This Guide

Validation Protocol for Field Researchers

Confidence Code System

CodeMeaningWhen Used
L Low confidence — single secondary source, unverified PSA national data used as proxy; no local data available
M Medium — triangulated across 2+ secondary sources or 1 admin KII This guide's pre-filled answers are code M
H High — community-validated but not yet at townhall After FGD or individual KII confirms the data
C Community-validated — confirmed at data return townhall After community townhall session; highest authority

How to Code Field Responses

✓ CONVERGENT
Community response aligns with this guide. Note it, move on. It confirms the secondary data at code M → upgrading toward H.
≠ DIVERGENT
Community response contradicts or significantly nuances this guide. This is your most valuable data. Document verbatim. Do NOT correct the participant to match this guide.
★ EMERGENT
Community raises something entirely new — not in this guide, not in ECSOM secondary data. Document carefully. Goes into the CEESAM Data Gap Register (Instrument 08, Sheet 5).

A number that contradicts PSA data
→ CEESAM Data Gap Register · Sheet 5 'Divergences'
A new livelihood or resource type not in the matrix
→ CEESAM instrument · new row/column proposal to LAB.PH coordinator
A governance actor not in the Governance Map
→ Governance Map worksheet · add to appropriate Power-Interest quadrant

→ Field journal entry + Photovoice caption if photographable + ECSOM narrative note

This guide contains starting points derived from secondary data. The community's lived knowledge is always more current, more precise, and more authoritative than any document produced in an office. Your primary job in the field is to listen, not to confirm.
